\section{Introduction}
This report presents the design, topology optimization, and structural verification of the new transom and motor mounting assembly developed for the 2026--2027 season.

Our primary objective was to lower the motor mount by $11\text{ cm}$ relative to the previous configuration to match the hull's updated hydrodynamic requirements, while simultaneously reducing the overall structural mass. Through an iterative workflow combining topology optimization and finite element analysis (FEM), we identified the need to introduce a \textbf{third lower anchor point} linked via two reinforcement struts to the mounts on the forward longitudinal tubes, as the bending moment generated by motor thrust proved excessive.

\reportfig{Assieme_specchio.png}{CAD assembly view of the lowered transom with the third lower anchor point and reinforcement struts to the longitudinal tubes.}{fig:assieme_cad}

This architecture closes the load path for propeller-thrust bending moments directly into the longitudinal tubes, reducing transom deflection and enabling a significant mass reduction of the primary plate.

\section{Structural Analysis and FEM Considerations}
\subsection{Assumptions and FEM Load Definition}
To validate the structural integrity of the new assembly prior to manufacturing, finite element simulations (FEM) were performed using SolidWorks Design. To maintain a high operational safety margin and prevent failure in rough sea states or aggressive maneuvers, conservative loads and boundary conditions were adopted:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Propeller thrust:} a maximum longitudinal force of $2000\text{ N}$ applied at the propeller hub, generating a substantial bending moment on the transom due to the increased lever arm from the $11\text{ cm}$ drop.
    \item \textbf{Vertical load (motor weight and inertia):} a static vertical load of $500\text{ N}$ representing the powertrain mass, amplified by a dynamic factor to account for wave slam loads.
    \item \textbf{Support reactions:} thrust-induced loads are distributed across all three anchor points, producing resultant forces up to $4400\text{--}5000\text{ N}$ at the longitudinal tube connections and the lower third anchor point.
    \item \textbf{Boundary conditions:} the forward longitudinal tube mounts and main transom mounting seats were constrained as pinned or fixed supports according to the degrees of freedom permitted by the rod ends.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Analytical Load Analysis of the Assembly}
To establish sizing loads for the structural components and reinforcement struts, a static equilibrium analysis of the motor-transom assembly was carried out.

Considering a vertical motor weight of $500\text{ N}$ acting at a $150\text{ mm}$ moment arm and a propeller thrust of $2000\text{ N}$ at a height of $1000\text{ mm}$, moment equilibrium about the upper mounts yields the horizontal reaction force $H$ on the struts:
\begin{equation}
H = \frac{500\text{ N} \cdot 150\text{ mm} + 2000\text{ N} \cdot 1000\text{ mm}}{2 \cdot 310\text{ mm}} \approx 3350\text{ N}
\end{equation}

Projecting the forces along the inclined struts (triangulation geometry of $464\text{ mm}$ and $310\text{ mm}$):
\begin{align}
R &= H \cdot \frac{464}{345} \approx 4505\text{ N} \\
V &= H \cdot \frac{310}{345} \approx 3010\text{ N}
\end{align}

For the lower third anchor point and its struts ($C = 5050\text{ N}$, $L = 2282\text{ N}$):
\begin{align}
B &= C \cdot \frac{390}{520} \approx 3788\text{ N} \\
A &= C \cdot \frac{345}{520} \approx 3350\text{ N}
\end{align}

Estimating a static friction coefficient ($\mu_s = 0.35$) at the mating interfaces, retention capacity is satisfied; the clamping/holding force limit is:
\begin{equation}
F = \frac{A}{\mu_s} = \frac{3350\text{ N}}{0.35} \approx 9571\text{ N} < B
\end{equation}

\section{Conclusions}
The new transom developed for the 2026--2027 season meets the design requirements. Introducing the lower third anchor point and reinforcement struts to the forward longitudinal tubes allowed us to lower the motor mount by $11\text{ cm}$ while reducing transom mass by 39\% and lowering deflections under load, maintaining adequate safety margins in operation.
